Motor-vehicle-parts safe-harbor wording vs methods

Passenger & OHV parts · Content vs channels · Not legal advice

California Prop 65 discussions mix two ideas that are easy to conflate: the exact words OEHHA publishes for a product category, and the places those words must appear for each way you sell. For motor-vehicle parts, that split matters because the same sentence might need to live on a counter sign, a label sheet, and an eBay listing — depending on your channels.

What “safe-harbor wording” means here

OEHHA maintains tailored safe-harbor warnings for certain product types. For passenger or off-highway motor vehicle parts, the operative English sentence is in 27 CCR § 25607.51 (Article 6, motor vehicle parts, operative January 1, 2025 on the site’s product materials). The sentence describes handling parts, phthalates and lead, cancer and reproductive harm, basic PPE habits, and points readers to p65warnings.ca.gov/motor-vehicle-parts.

Using the published sentence — with required formatting such as the word WARNING and the yellow triangle symbol in 27 CCR § 25603(a)(1) where applicable — is what people mean by “safe-harbor content.” PartsHarbor Shop Kit PDFs and listing snippets mirror that public text; they do not invent alternate chemical lists for posted signs.

Official safe-harbor text (27 CCR § 25607.51) Handling passenger or off-highway motor vehicle parts can expose you to chemicals such as phthalates and lead, which can cause cancer and reproductive harm. To minimize exposure, service the vehicle in a well-ventilated area, wear gloves, and wash your hands. For more information see www.P65Warnings.ca.gov/motor-vehicle-parts.

What “warning methods” means

Methods are delivery channels — how the buyer or handler sees the content:

  • Posted warnings at a California parts counter or rack (see posted sign vs internet guide)
  • On-product or shelf labels applied to parts or packaging in the supply chain
  • Internet methods under 27 CCR § 25602(b) for product display pages, catalogs, and marketplaces
  • Manufacturer → retailer notice paperwork under 27 CCR § 25600.2 when you supply other sellers (included in PartsHarbor’s notice template as ops paperwork, not legal advice)

Article 6 sections such as § 25607.50(a)(3) tie methods together — for example, when a part is sold on the internet, subsection (B) references internet methods under § 25602(b) in addition to posted warnings where posted methods are used.

Passenger cars, trucks, motorcycles, ATV/UTV, salvage yards

OEHHA’s motor-vehicle-parts category is meant to cover the shared supply chain for passenger and off-highway parts rather than a different sentence per vehicle type. Your ops question is usually category fit (is this SKU a covered “part”?) plus channel fit (where do we warn?). Exclusions in 27 CCR § 25607.50(b) — tires, packaged service chemicals, asbestos parts, carpeting, upholstery, textiles, fabrics — are the first place to check if a SKU feels “automotive” but is not in the tailored parts lane.

Safe harbor is not a product test

Safe-harbor text answers “if I must warn, what words satisfy the published method?” It does not answer “does this caliper / harness / plastic trim require a warning?” Exposure, supplier declarations, and product chemistry sit upstream of implementation. PartsHarbor sells implementation files; our Weekly Watch digest reports public OAG 60-day notice themes for context, not SKU-level determinations.

Putting content and methods on a worksheet

  1. Confirm the SKU is in scope for motor-vehicle parts (and not in § 25607.50(b) exclusions) using OEHHA pages and your supplier data.
  2. List every California sales path: counter, ship-from-store, eBay, Amazon, dealer portal, etc.
  3. Assign a method per path (posted, label, internet § 25602, notice trail) using the same § 25607.51 sentence where the safe harbor applies.
  4. Review bilingual needs if your location uses Spanish consumer signage elsewhere.

FAQ

Can I edit the safe-harbor sentence?

Posted safe-harbor signs use the exact published English sentence; paraphrases or chemical-name swaps break the safe-harbor content for posted methods. Internet methods still require the published text at the linked destination.

Is Spanish required?

Bilingual requirements depend on method and location context under OEHHA rules. PartsHarbor includes EN/ES label sheets; evaluate posted and internet needs against current OEHHA guidance for your signage program.

Where do I read the official rules?

Start at p65warnings.ca.gov/motor-vehicle-parts and the linked CCR sections (25607.50–25607.51, 25602, 25603). Cross-check operative dates when OEHHA updates text.
