Posted 5×5 sign vs internet listing warning

Counter / POS vs catalog & marketplace · Not legal advice

Search teams often land on “Prop 65 posted sign” or “5×5 Prop 65 parts” when setting up a counter, then discover the same SKUs also sell on eBay, Amazon, or a Shopify catalog. For passenger and off-highway motor vehicle parts, California publishes a tailored safe-harbor path — but posted methods and internet methods are different implementation channels. This page explains that split in plain ops language.

Posted warnings: the counter, rack, or POS area

When customers physically browse parts in California — at a counter, salvage yard office, powersports rack, or similar — a posted warning method can be part of the safe-harbor toolkit for motor-vehicle parts. OEHHA describes posted formatting (including the yellow triangle symbol in 27 CCR § 25603(a)(1) and the word WARNING) together with the exact English sentence in 27 CCR § 25607.51.

Many shops use a roughly 5×5 inch sign at the point where parts are handled or selected. That layout is a practical print target, not a substitute for thinking through every sales channel. If your location uses bilingual consumer signage elsewhere, review whether Spanish materials are also appropriate for your posted warnings.

PartsHarbor’s free checklist section 1 is built for this channel: “Do customers see parts on a counter, rack, or showroom floor in California?”

Internet and catalog warnings: listings buyers read before paying

When the same inventory sells online to California buyers, OEHHA’s internet warning methods in 27 CCR § 25602 apply to the product display path — eBay descriptions, Amazon product pages, WooCommerce/Shopify templates, and wholesale catalog pages that function as pre-purchase product displays.

Common implementation patterns include:

  • Showing the full § 25607.51 sentence on the product display page, or
  • Using a clearly marked hyperlink on that page with anchor text limited to WARNING, CA WARNING, or CALIFORNIA WARNING (per § 25602(b)(1)(B)), linking to a destination that displays the safe-harbor sentence.

A theme-level site footer or generic store policy page is a weak stand-in for SKU-level placement — marketplace audits usually focus on what a buyer sees on the listing itself. See eBay listing vs packing slip for post-checkout paperwork vs pre-purchase listings.

When you need both channels

If you operate a California counter and sell the same parts on the internet, the published motor-vehicle-parts text in 27 CCR § 25607.50(a)(3)(B) describes using internet methods under § 25602(b) in addition to posted warnings — not choosing one instead of the other. That is the regulatory concept behind the everyday shop rule: print the sign for the desk; paste the HTML for the listing.

Internet-only sellers may never print a 5×5 sign, but they still need a § 25602 method on each relevant product path. Counter-only sellers who never ship out of state still have their own scope questions — this guide focuses on the common hybrid: yard + eBay + Amazon.

Catalog / B2B nuance

Wholesale PDF catalogs and password-protected dealer portals are still “internet” or remote sales paths in many ops reviews when they show product pages before order confirmation. Treat distributor catalog SKUs like marketplace listings: each line needs a visible WARNING path appropriate to that medium, not only a sign at your will-call window.

Official safe-harbor text (27 CCR § 25607.51) Handling passenger or off-highway motor vehicle parts can expose you to chemicals such as phthalates and lead, which can cause cancer and reproductive harm. To minimize exposure, service the vehicle in a well-ventilated area, wear gloves, and wash your hands. For more information see www.P65Warnings.ca.gov/motor-vehicle-parts.

FAQ

Does my 5×5 counter sign cover online sales?

When the same motor-vehicle parts are sold on the internet, § 25607.50(a)(3)(B) points to internet methods under § 25602(b) in addition to posted warnings. Plan separate assets for counter and listings.

What belongs on the posted sign?

Use OEHHA’s published posted method formatting and the exact § 25607.51 English sentence — not a paraphrase or chemical list substitution on posted signs.

What belongs on the website or marketplace listing?

A § 25602(b) internet method — full sentence on the product display page and/or a compliant WARNING hyperlink — visible before purchase.
